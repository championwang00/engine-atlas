Direct-injection diesel

A direct-injection diesel sprays fuel into hot compressed air in the main chamber, often using a shaped piston bowl. Direct injection describes location; common rail, distributor pumps and unit injectors describe fuel-system architecture.

Fiat histories date the Croma milestone to 1986, 1987 or 1988, so the display retains “late 1980s.” Audi documents the 1989 IAA debut of the , combining direct injection, turbocharging and electronic management. Avoiding a separate prechamber can reduce losses, but spray formation, noise, soot and NOx need careful control. Injection pressure alone does not ensure clean exhaust.

In 2006 the V12-powered became the first diesel car to win the Le Mans 24 Hours overall. Its programme brought diesel injection, combustion and reliability development into top-level endurance racing; Audi’s contemporary report also described links between racing and production research. The result belongs to its regulations, vehicle design and race conditions, rather than proving a universal advantage for every diesel engine.
